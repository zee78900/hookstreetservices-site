This is the cash that has already come back: $199,900 of the $1,250,000 put in, which is 16.0% of the money, across a weighted hold of about 3.9 years.
The step up from 16.0% in real cash to +27% on paper is not cash yet, and the paper is uneven in quality. Of the five reported values:
  • 1 of the 5 is a 409A tax valuation, a figure prepared for stock-option paperwork, not what a buyer would pay.
  • 1 of the 5 is loan principal under a signed forbearance deal, meaning the borrower got extra time on new written terms.
  • 3 of the 5 are current statements, from the fund administrator, the sponsor, and the custodian.
Every figure here is arithmetic on the owner's own reported statements and cash records. It is not Hook Street's performance and not an independent valuation.

Needs attention

Three items this quarter

December 31, 2026 is the biggest date on the calendar act
The Harborview note is being paid under a signed forbearance deal (May 2, 2026), two payments of $3,000 in and both on time, but only the balloon itself, the full $150,000 due in one payment at year-end, ends the risk. Ashford can also still ask for its final $30,000 through December 2026, so both cash events land in the same month.
Harborview is second in line, not first verify
The title search completed April 30, 2026 confirms the lien but shows it sits behind a $950,000 first mortgage: if the property ever had to be sold to collect, that $950,000 gets paid before this note sees a dollar. The original loan file described a first-position lien. The downside is thinner than previously assumed.
Northline's paper gain rests on a 409A figure weigh
The $500,000 stake value is a 409A valuation dated December 31, 2025, a figure companies get for stock-option paperwork, not what a buyer has offered. Distributions have begun ($12,000, the first ever) but one payment does not establish the planned $60,000 to $80,000 per year.

No holding carries the red "past due / impaired" tag this quarter. Harborview carried it in the Q1 2026 brief and moved to amber when the forbearance was signed.

Send-ready questions

Three questions to ask now

Written to go out as they are, each to the person who can answer it.

  1. Harborview counsel: please confirm receipt of each monthly $3,000 payment as it lands, and confirm exactly how the December 31, 2026 balloon payoff will work, including release of the lien once it is paid. (largest single date on the calendar)
  2. Northline management: please send FY2025 financials and the distribution policy in writing. Is $12,000 per quarter the intended rate, and what determines it? What are the terms of the raise under discussion? (payment pace and the risk of the stake shrinking, both unproven)
  3. Ashford administrator: when do you expect to ask for the final $30,000, and what is the wind-down schedule to the November 2027 fund end? (December cash planning)
