Sample Owner Brief: five holdings, Q2-2026

Prepared for: Meridian Family Holdings (fictional) Period: Q2-2026 (2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30) As of: 2026-06-30 · Prepared: 2026-07-20 Prior brief: Q1-2026 (2026-04-18) Prepared by: Sam Treitel, Hook Street
What this is

A factual, sourced picture of what you own and how it is actually doing.

This is the fourth quarterly brief for this fictional client. Each quarter it reconstructs the factual position of the five holdings: what was committed and contributed, what has actually come back in cash, what was originally promised and by when, the latest reported value with its source and date, what changed since the last brief, what to watch next, and at most one action per holding. Every figure carries its source and as-of date. Where something cannot be sourced, it is marked unknown, not estimated.

This brief does

  • Organize your own supplied documents
  • Record reported values with source and date
  • Calculate cash in, cash out, and return % on your own reported figures
  • Compare actual events to the original written expectations
  • Grade each sponsor's reporting quality
  • Prepare factual questions for your professionals

This brief does not

  • Recommend buying, selling, holding, or allocating
  • Select securities or manage money
  • Give an independent fair-value opinion
  • Raise capital or place investments
  • Charge on assets, performance, or transactions
  • Replace your accountant, attorney, or adviser

Here is what your money did: of the $1,250,000 you put in, $199,900 (16.0%, about +4.1%/yr) has actually come back in cash. Counting the latest reported values, the five holdings stand at $1,587,000 of total value, about 1.27x your money (+27% total, roughly +6.9%/yr averaged). The step up from 16% in cash to 27% on paper is not cash yet, and the paper is uneven in quality: one value comes from a 409A tax valuation (a figure prepared for stock-option paperwork, not what a buyer would pay), one is loan principal under a signed forbearance deal (the borrower got extra time on new written terms), and three are current statements. These figures are arithmetic on your own reported statements and cash records, not Hook Street's performance and not an independent valuation.
How the numbers are computed. Cash back = cash actually received ÷ cash put in (the figure fund managers call DPI). Total on paper = (cash received + latest reported value) ÷ cash put in. Per-year figures = total return % ÷ years held: simple division, not compounding, and not an IRR (the fancier yearly-return math funds publish). The blended average counts bigger and longer-held investments more; weighted that way, the five holdings have been held about 3.9 years. Where a fund reports its own IRR, it is shown as the fund's figure and is not recomputed. All arithmetic runs on the owner's reported figures and inherits their bases and as-of dates.
