“Cedar Row Property Management”: 12 units, one owner, everything by phone and text

Start with intake and a calendar, not a robot.

Every maintenance request lands as a text on your personal phone and nothing gets logged, while 12 leases end in eight different months with no date the system knows. Your bottleneck isn’t reply speed, it’s that nothing is written down and nothing has a deadline on it, so fix those two first and AI drafting has something real to work with.

Three moves, ranked by payoff against effort

Assumes 15 to 20 tenant texts a week and about one move-out a quarter; half the volume, half the savings. These are hours, not revenue. Nobody should promise you revenue from an intake sheet.

Three things not to automate

  • Anything that reaches a tenant without your eyes. Drafts yes, auto-send no. Your relationships are the business, and one wrong automated message costs more than every minute saved.
  • Emergencies. No heat in winter, gas smell, water where it shouldn’t be: that’s a phone call and, in most states, a legal duty with a clock on it. Keep a human line that skips every workflow, and tell tenants exactly what it is.
  • Decisions about people. Screening, deposit deductions, hardship calls. AI can assemble the file, the photos, the ledger. The decision, and the paper trail showing you applied the same standard to everyone, stays with you.

The four-week starter plan

  • Week 1. Business number live, old one forwarded, plus one hour of typing to get all 12 lease end dates into one tab. Done when every tenant text lands in one inbox and every lease has a date the system knows.
  • Week 2. Wire the intake log: unit, issue, date, status, urgency tag, all captured automatically. Done when a full week of requests sorts itself without you typing any of it.
  • Week 3. Turn on drafting behind your review: replies, renewal letters, notices. Done when you’re approving, not composing, and the first renewal nudge has fired on its own.
  • Week 4. Run month-end through the money page and start the contractor tally. Done when one full month’s money checks out in under 15 minutes.

Tools, roughly what they cost

Roughly $35 to $70 a month all in: a business number (OpenPhone, ~$15 to $20), a paid Claude or ChatGPT plan for the drafting (~$20), Zapier or Make to move each text into the log (free tier to ~$30), and Google Sheets, which you already have. If someone quotes you $500 a month for this, ask exactly what the extra $430 buys.

Nothing here needs me to work. Start week 1 and measure, and if the intake log isn’t saving you real hours by week 3, stop and call before spending another dollar.
