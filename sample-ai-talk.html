Start with intake, not a robot

Your bottleneck isn’t that replies take too long, it’s that nothing gets logged. Fix the pipe first and the AI has something to work with. The three moves below run in that order.

What we heard

The setup: 12 units across three small buildings. One owner, we’ll call him Danny (invented name, like everything else on this page), plus a part-time handyman. No office, no software, no staff.

The day-to-day: every maintenance request, rent question, and lease conversation arrives as a text or call on Danny’s personal phone. Nothing is logged anywhere. When a tenant says “I told you about this last month,” there is no record to check.

The money: rent comes in by check and Zelle. Month-end reconciliation means scrolling the bank app and trying to remember who’s paid.

The real question under the question: Danny asked if AI could answer his tenants for him. It can draft, and that helps a lot. But full auto-reply is the wrong first move for a 12-unit operation built on personal relationships, and we talked through why.

What not to automate

  • Anything that reaches a tenant without your eyes. Drafts yes, auto-send no. Your relationships are the business; a wrong automated message costs more than the minutes it saves.
  • Emergencies. A burst pipe is a phone call. Keep a human path that skips every workflow, and tell tenants exactly what it is.
  • Late-rent and hardship conversations. AI can prep the facts and the history. The judgment call stays yours.
  • A full property-management platform, for now. At 12 units the migration costs more than it saves. Revisit around 25 units, after intake is running clean.
