The factoring offer, priced honestly

The offer as quoted: 85% advance on invoice face, a fee of 2.5% of face for the first 30 days, then 1.0% for each additional 10 days or part thereof, $350 due-diligence fee at signing, $25 per wire, 12-month term with a $15,000 monthly minimum in factored volume. Your AR aging says customers actually pay in about 46 days. Per $100,000 of invoices:

  1. Advance wired on day 1 (85% of face): $85,000
  2. Fee at a 46-day collection: 2.5% (days 1-30) + 1.0% (days 31-40) + 1.0% (days 41-46, a partial block charged in full) = 4.5% of face = $4,500
  3. Rebate when your customer pays: $15,000 held back, minus the $4,500 fee = $10,500
  4. So the cost is $4,500 for the use of $85,000 for 46 days
  5. Period rate: $4,500 ÷ $85,000 = 5.29% per 46 days
  6. Annualized: 5.29% × (365 ÷ 46) = about 42% effective APR (simple annualization; compounding and the fixed fees push it higher)
Even in the best case, a customer paying inside 30 days, the math is 2.5% of face on an 85% advance = 2.94% per 30 days, or about 36% a year. The quoted “2.5%” is a 30-day price on the face amount, not a yearly rate on the money you actually receive. The $15,000 monthly minimum also means fees keep accruing whether or not you use the facility.

Three readiness gaps a lender will flag

  • Nine negative-balance days in 90: underwriters read this as cash stress regardless of revenue. It is the single most common quiet decline reason on bank lines.
  • 54% of receivables sit with one contractor: a factor will carve out or cap that account, which guts the usable advance; a bank will haircut the borrowing base for the same reason.
  • The books do not tie to the bank: a $130K gap between P&L revenue and deposits, no current debt schedule in the file, and the 2025 return still on extension. What a lender cannot verify, a lender declines.

What to fix before applying (about 45 days)

  • Run 60 clean days of statements: move personal charges out of the business account and hold a buffer so there are zero negative days. This one change moves the pricing tier.
  • Tie the books out: reconcile the $130K books-to-bank gap with a one-page written explanation, and put a current AR aging and debt schedule in the application file.
  • De-risk the concentration on paper: a signed contract and written payment terms with the general contractor, and deposits on new jobs, shrink the concentration story even before the number moves.
  • Sequence the ask: apply for the equipment line first, while no blanket UCC lien exists to block it. Treat factoring, if at all, as a defined short bridge for one or two slow payers, not standing working capital.

Questions to ask any provider, in writing

  • Is the fee charged on the invoice face or on the advance, and are the time tiers pro-rated or charged in full blocks?
  • Recourse or non-recourse, and what exactly happens on day 90 if my customer has not paid?
  • Contract length, monthly minimum, termination fee, and does it auto-renew?
  • Will you file a blanket UCC-1, and will it block my equipment financing?
  • Who contacts my customers, and what does the notification letter say?
  • State the all-in effective APR at my actual 46-day collection speed, in writing.
